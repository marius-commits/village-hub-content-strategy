3 · Weekly rhythm

A week the floor can actually keep.

Four public beats, not an eight-week grid. Sunday stays dark. Nothing goes out between 20:00 and 07:00 SAST. If the week is thin on the floor, we skip a beat rather than invent a crowd.

Mon

Internal: captions to Christel, CC Marius. Confirm prices against the live site. Check the room diary with Hub Desk.

Tue

Meet the floor. Tenant introduction (once there is a signed yes), plus a Tuesday coffee story. This is the community heartbeat.

Wed

Quiet on feed. Stories only if there is a real tour, a real room gap, or a real moment on the floor.

Thu

The offer. Alternate the 2-seater (O1) with a desk or room offer (D1, D2, B1). One clear price, one clear CTA.

Fri

Stories from the floor. Foundry lawn when the weather is kind. Recap, not a hard sell.

Sat

Village Stay, every second Saturday — midweek work trip one week, weekend stay the next.

Sun

No marketing. The floor rests, and so does the feed.

5 · Channel roles

Each channel has a job. We will not post the same caption everywhere for the sake of it.

Instagram

The main public face. Feed for finished plays (desks, 2-seater, rooms once photographed, tenant intros). Stories for Tuesday coffee, Friday lawn, and real-time room gaps. Reels reserved for the walk-through once we have a real clip.

Facebook

Mirrors the finished feed posts, with slightly fuller captions for an older Stellenbosch audience. Useful for Stay and for event-adjacent community posts, without inventing events we do not host.

LinkedIn

Quiet and professional: 2-seater offices, boardrooms, virtual office, and tenant intros when the tenant wants to be seen there. Sixty-three followers and a long silence means quality over frequency — a post every two weeks is enough until the page has a pulse again.

Google Business Profile

The 2-seater, the two rooms (once photographed), and the tour CTA. This is where practical searches convert. Keep photos honest and hours correct. Do not use it to argue with other venues.

WhatsApp is not a broadcast channel in this plan. Member messages, review asks and follow-ups live in the WhatsApp strategy and the shared inbox. Marketing may point people to +27 63 260 3513; it does not send campaigns from that line.

8 · Who does what, and the sign-off

A person still presses Post.

HUB Marketing drafts the plan, the captions and the visual briefs. Christel is the floor driver and the person who posts. Marius is notified, and anything that spends money, changes the site or changes member contact waits for his written yes.

HUB Marketing

Owns outbound content and posting strategy for Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, Google Business Profile, and anything else that goes out in public. Drafts this page, the captions and the shoot list. Does not auto-publish.

Christel

Approves and posts every social caption. Hosts Tuesday coffee and tenant conversations on the floor. First eyes on a low rating or a difficult comment. The public never sees a post she has not accepted.

Marius

Signs off spend, ads, site changes and anything that touches member contact beyond Hub Desk’s standing work. Is copied on caption emails. Is notified, not asked to post.

Before the first week goes live

  • Christel agrees the weekly rhythm and the first two captions (a Tuesday floor moment, and a Thursday 2-seater or day-pass offer).
  • A real photograph of a 2-seater is in hand, or we hold O1 and lead with D1 on a real open-floor photo instead.
  • Boardroom and Chamber plays stay in draft until real room photos exist.
  • At least one tenant has said yes in writing before we announce a weekly introduction series.
  • Prices are checked against the live pricing page on the day the caption is sent.
  • Village Stay listing facts (children, check-in, enquiry path) are agreed before S1/S2 are pushed hard.
  • Caption email goes to Christel, CC Marius, from the Hub side — ready to copy, not as a strategy essay.

Hub Desk remains the operations owner (Spacebring, occupancy, the WhatsApp member journey). This page is the marketing companion to that work, not a second operations plan.

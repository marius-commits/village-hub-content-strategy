Moving a Team of Four to Six Into One Office in Stellenbosch: A Practical Checklist (2026)

Most small teams in Stellenbosch don’t decide to get an office. They drift into needing one. Four or five people work from home, a café or a spare room, and a WhatsApp group does the job of a corridor. It works until it doesn’t.

This checklist is for that moment: a team of four to six that is ready to sit together, and wants to get the move right the first time.

Five signs your team needs one room

  • Client meetings happen in coffee shops. It’s fine for a first chat. It isn’t fine when you need a screen, privacy or a signature.
  • New people have nowhere to sit. Onboarding over video is slow, and a new hire learns most by sitting next to someone who already knows the job.
  • Equipment lives in cars. Laptops, samples and files travel because there’s nowhere safe to leave them.
  • Your business address is someone’s home. It’s on the invoices, the website and the Google profile. (If an address is all you need for now, a business address may be the better first step.)
  • You plan around each other’s kitchens. “Whose house this week?” is a sign the team has outgrown it.

The checklist: what to ask before you sign anything

1. Seats now, and in twelve months

Count the people who will be in most days, then add the hire you’re planning next. A six-seat room for a team of four gives you headroom without moving again in six months.

2. Parking for every car

In Stellenbosch this decides more office moves than the rent does. Ask how many bays you get, whether they’re free, and where visitors park.

3. Meeting rooms on demand, not every day

A team of six rarely needs a boardroom every day, but it needs one on the day a client visits. Book by the hour instead of paying for a room you use twice a month.

4. Internet you don’t have to manage

Ask who fixes the Wi-Fi when it drops, and whether your team is on its own network.

5. Access hours

Early starts, late finishes and weekend deadlines happen. Ask whether you can get in at 06:00 or on a Saturday without phoning anyone.

6. What’s actually inside the monthly number

Furniture, cleaning, electricity, water, Wi-Fi, coffee, printing, reception. With a conventional lease, many of these are extra, and there can be a fit-out and a deposit on top. Our office-to-rent guide has a full true-cost checklist.

7. Term and notice

Ask how long you commit for, and what happens if the team grows or shrinks. Get the answer in writing.

8. The address on your invoices

Your office address becomes your business address. Check it reads well on a quote and on Google.

A simple move-in plan

  1. View it with the whole team. Sit in the chairs, check the light, walk from the car to the door.
  2. Agree the start date and the term in writing.
  3. Sort access for everyone who needs to get in.
  4. Bring laptops and files. The office is furnished, so the move is mostly bags, not bakkies. (Unfurnished is possible on request.)
  5. Book your first client meeting in the Chamber or the Boardroom, and update the address on your invoices and Google profile.
